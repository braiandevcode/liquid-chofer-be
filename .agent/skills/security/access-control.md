## ACCESS_CONTROL

### RULES
- ALWAYS examine and verify that session management and authorization are correctly validated on the server, preventing:
    - unauthorized access to other users' resources (IDOR/BOLA, horizontal escalation) and to higher-privilege functions (vertical escalation, admin routes, unprotected HTTP methods), including forced browsing and manipulation of parameters such as `role`, `is_admin`, or `user_id` (mass assignment)
    - poorly managed tokens (JWT, access token, and refresh token): validate signature, algorithm (reject `none`), `exp`, `iss`, and `aud`; use short-lived access tokens; rotate the refresh token on every use and detect its reuse (invalidating the whole token family); invalidate on the server on logout, password change, or detected compromise, so that an old, revoked, or expired token is never accepted as valid
    - insecure token storage and transport: prefer `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, and `SameSite` cookies; do not expose them in URLs, logs, error messages, API responses, or in `localStorage` if there is XSS risk
    - flaws in the registration, verification, and login flow: user enumeration (different messages and response times), brute force without attempt limits, predictable, reusable, non-expiring, or URL-exposed verification or reset tokens, session fixation (regenerate the session ID after authentication), skipping flow steps (verification or MFA), and account takeover through email or password change without reauthentication
    - IDOR in any process that requires a session (view, edit, delete, download, or share resources such as images, files, orders, or profiles): validate on every request that the resource belongs to the authenticated user or that the user has explicit permission, in route parameters, query, body, headers, and also in WebSockets and GraphQL
- Apply the deny-by-default principle: authorization is always decided on the server, at every endpoint and for every object, through a centralized and reusable layer (middleware or policies), and never just by hiding buttons or routes in the frontend.
- Do not use predictable identifiers as the only protection: UUIDs or random IDs help, but they do not replace ownership verification. To share resources, use signed links with expiration, limited scope, and revocability.
- Reinforce with defense in depth: rate limits and temporary lockout on login and account recovery, MFA for sensitive actions, reauthentication on critical changes, least privilege per role, and logging and monitoring of denied accesses and enumeration patterns.
- If a vulnerability is detected: fix it, test with abuse cases (access another user's resources with one user's session by changing IDs, use an expired, revoked, or already-rotated token, reuse a refresh token, skip steps of the registration or login flow, access without a session and with a lower role, and manipulate role parameters) to confirm it has been mitigated, and report to me the finding, its location, the cause, and the fix applied.
